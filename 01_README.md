# Pfizer's AI Transformation: From Pharmaceutical Giant to AI-Enabled Medicine Platform

A digital-transformation case study built with agentic AI tools (Claude Cowork) for the ITEC-617 Information and Technology course at American University, Kogod School of Business.

> **Core case question:** Can Pfizer use AI to improve productivity, accelerate innovation, and strengthen patient engagement while managing regulatory, data, ethical, and organizational risks?

## About this project

This repository contains a Harvard Business School (HBS)-style mini case study on Pfizer's enterprise-wide AI transformation. The case is protagonist-centered, grounded in primary sources (Pfizer's 2025 Annual Review, the Q4 2025 earnings call, the 2025 AI Festival announcement, FDA draft guidance, and 30+ secondary sources), and ends with six discussion questions meant to generate genuine debate rather than a tidy resolution.

The protagonist is **Lidia Fonseca**, Pfizer's Executive Vice President and Chief Digital and Technology Officer. **Albert Bourla**, Pfizer's Chairman and CEO, is the strategic / board-facing voice. The DT initiative is Pfizer's enterprise-wide AI buildout — 1,200+ GPUs, an internal generative AI platform estimated at $750M–$1B in near-term value, AI-driven manufacturing throughput gains of ~20%, supercomputing that has cut computational research times by 80–90%, agentic workflows for clinical-trial site selection, and a workforce-mobilization vehicle (the AI Festival) that puts Mistral AI's Arthur Mensch and Scale AI's Jason Droege in front of Pfizer's ~81,000 colleagues.

## Why this case is strategically interesting

Pfizer's COVID franchise has collapsed from $56.7B (2022) to ~$6.7B (2025), and ~$17B more in annual revenue is at risk by 2030 from loss of exclusivity on Eliquis and Ibrance. The $7.2B cost-savings program by 2026 — and the $1.5B in manufacturing optimization through 2027 — depend on AI carrying a meaningful share of the productivity load. The case is built around four genuinely unresolved tensions: productivity vs. innovation; build vs. partner; speed vs. regulatory exposure; and workforce transformation.

## How the case was built

This project followed the assignment's recommended Cowork workflow: source review, drafting, verification, and reflection — with verification treated as a discrete pass rather than as cleanup.

1. **Source plan.** Started with a curated 11-source plan (Pfizer Annual Review, Q4 2025 earnings transcript, AI Festival announcement, Reuters coverage, McKinsey reports).
2. **Verification before drafting.** Ran 10+ targeted single-claim web searches to corroborate every key figure (1,200+ GPUs, $7.2B / $1.5B savings, $750M–$1B GenAI platform value, 80–90% computational time reduction, $56.7B 2022 COVID franchise revenue, etc.) before writing.
3. **Iterative drafting.** Document 1 first (Main Case), then Document 2 (Technical Supplement), then Document 3 (Additional Sources, including the data-points table).
4. **Step 5 verification pass.** Walked Document 3's data table top to bottom and resolved every "verify" status. Caught one material error (Pfizer headcount — corrected from "ninety thousand" to the verified ~81,000), upgraded three Bourla quotes from paraphrases to verified direct quotes from the 2025 Annual Review CEO letter, and demoted one paraphrase that had been mis-attributed.
5. **Two-page reflection report.** Written last, grounded in concrete examples from the AI usage log.

## What I learned (short version)

The most important discipline was **verifying numbers before drafting**, not after. The single biggest catch from the verification pass was an inflated headcount figure that survived three drafts — exactly the kind of plausible-sounding-but-wrong number the assignment warns about. The case I am submitting is one I can defend line by line. Where a claim could not survive primary-source verification, I either rewrote it, demoted it from a quote to a paraphrase, or cited it as a range rather than a point estimate.
